 Learning to interpret user needs and user stories.
User needs interpretation begins with looking at what the user wants to achieve not by mere features. This is translated in software development as general requirements are converted into specific actionable goals that are used in design and implementation. In this process, user stories come in particularly handy since they can be used to decompose requirements into simple statements. This format makes the development process user-friendly and makes each feature have a clear purpose. Prioritization of work, finding of acceptance criteria and ensuring that the final product is an actual solution to the problem of the user are also easier when using user stories, rather than merely executing the technical work.
2. Agile processes and program development.
Now when I think about program development, I think of it as a process, and not a program plan that should be followed step-by-step. I would have planned first, followed by little development, testing and feedback cycles, rather than attempting to create everything at once. Agile practices such as sprints, sprint planning, sprint reviews and retrospectives enable the development to be both flexible and structured. In future work, I would like to keep applying the sprint-based planning to divide big tasks into small manageable objectives and to use the ongoing feedback as one of the elements to enhance a product in the early phases instead of trying to fix it at the final stage. This methodology mitigates risk and enhances the total quality.
3. What being a good team member in software development means.
To be a good team player in software development is to be reliable, communicative and collaborative. On time completion of tasks is important but equally important is to report progress, challenge and change early enough so that the team can make amendments where necessary. Being a good team member also means that one listens to feedback and is ready to improve on the basis of group feedback. Teamwork is particularly relevant in Agile contexts as the success of the team is determined by continuous cooperation and collaborative responsibility. In general, a good member of a team does not only work individually but assists the team to be on track, organized, and targeted towards the project.
